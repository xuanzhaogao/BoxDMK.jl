Implementation notes

Free-space solver

The solver (src/native/) follows the nine steps of the Fortran bdmk. It keeps all of Fortran's numerical choices: SOG nodes, plane-wave quadratures and lengths, proxy orders, interaction lists, local tables and their sparsity patterns, and the dense-versus-sparse switch of the near field. That is why its results agree with Fortran to rounding. The speed comes from restructuring the work:

  • SIMD GEMM kernels (gemm.jl, SIMD.jl). Every tensor-product stage is a small register-blocked matrix product with no data transposes. The kernel runs at about the FP64 peak of one core (≈ 60 GFLOP/s on Zen 4), at or above OpenBLAS for these sizes.
  • Near field. Same-level neighbours are evaluated target-centrically, pot += w Σ_dz Tz(Σ_dy Ty(Σ_dx Tx f_s)), sharing the y/z stages among sources (about 2× fewer flops). Sparse pairs use GEMMs on pattern-zeroed tables.
  • Plane waves. Conjugate symmetry halves all complex transforms, which run as real GEMMs, and leaves form and evaluate plane waves directly from and to their grid values. The kernel's Fourier transform is folded into the outgoing expansions. The translation (M2L) runs over frequency blocks of all boxes, cache-blocked and partitioned over threads.
  • Tree passes. Parent↔child transfers share stages among the 8 children and fuse the leaf value↔proxy maps. Proxy data is stored only for nonleaf boxes.
  • Runtime. Subnormals are flushed to zero during the solve (Gaussian tails otherwise fall into slow subnormal arithmetic). Large buffers come from a malloc arena that is reused across solves, so they cause no GC pressure. Boxes are scheduled dynamically.

Periodic solver

src/native/periodic/:

  • forest.jl: PeriodicGrid (QR frame, level-0 grid and image halo) and build_periodic_forest. The forest uses Legendre tail refinement and 2:1 balance, with halo copies or core interpolation. Interpolated nodes reuse the previous node's core leaf when it holds their image, and are evaluated per leaf by three small products.
  • fractional.jl: the source tree in fractional coordinates for cells whose level-0 cubes do not tile the cell.
  • wide.jl: the wide-Gaussian transforms, moments → modes → Legendre coefficients. They only compute the half space $m_1 \ge 0$ (the density is real) and keep real and imaginary parts as separate arrays. Every contraction is a set of real GEMMs threaded over slices or row blocks, so OpenBLAS threads never compete with Julia's.
  • periodic.jl: the u-series SOG, the plan and the solve (narrow Gaussians through the free-space machinery, the wide term injected before the downward pass, the neutralizing background).

Repository layout

  • src/core/, src/tree/: types, bases, adaptive tree construction.
  • src/native/: the box-DMK solver; src/native/periodic/: the periodic solver.
  • src/solver/: bdmk, targets, PotentialField.
  • src/fortran/: optional wrappers of the Fortran reference (deps/boxdmk_fortran/).
  • benchmark/: native_vs_fortran.jl, periodic_vs_cpp.jl and node runners; benchmark/results/ holds the logs.
  • test/: the test suite. The Fortran parity tests run when the reference is built.

Limitations

  • The solvers are 3D only (the tree builder also supports 1D and 2D).
  • eps must lie in (1e-12, 1e-2], the range covered by the Fortran tables.
