Quasi-2D (slab) potentials

bdmk_q2d computes the Coulomb potential of a neutral density in a slab that is periodic in x and y (rectangle $L_1 \times L_2$) and free in z:

\[u(x) = \int \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}^2} \frac{f(y)}{|x - y - (n_1 L_1, n_2 L_2, 0)|}\,dy .\]

The density must vanish outside the slab $z_0 \le z \le z_1$ and have zero total charge (otherwise the 2-periodic sum diverges). The potential is computed inside the slab.

L = (100.0, 100.0)                       # or a number for a square cell
res = bdmk_q2d(f, L, (0.0, 1.0); eps = 1e-6)
evaluate_periodic(res, [12.3, 45.6, 0.5])

plan = q2d_plan(f0, L, (0.0, 1.0); eps = 1e-6)   # repeated solves
res = periodic_solve!(plan, f1)

Options are those of bdmk_periodic, and rc, the side of the level-0 boxes: $r_c \le \min(L_1, L_2, H)$ ($H = z_1 - z_0$), dividing both in-plane sides; by default the largest such value ($r_c = H$ for a thin slab, so the slab is one level-0 z-cell).

Method

The narrow Gaussians of the u-series (those that decay within rc) go through the real-space box machinery, as in the periodic solver, with image boxes in x and y only. The wide Gaussians are summed after Poisson summation over the in-plane lattice:

\[\sum_n e^{-|x - y - n\circ L|^2/\delta} = \frac{\pi\delta}{L_1 L_2} \sum_{\dot k} e^{-\delta|\dot k|^2/4} e^{i\dot k\cdot(\dot r - \dot r')} \, e^{-(z - z')^2/\delta}, \qquad \dot k = 2\pi(m_1/L_1, m_2/L_2).\]

  • In the plane, the transforms between Legendre moments of the level-0 cubes and the modes $\dot k$ are exact per-axis Legendre–spherical-Bessel expansions (half space $m_1 \ge 0$), as in the periodic solver. They are factored through the uniform grid of cells: with $c_s = (s+\tfrac12) r_c$ and $k = 2\pi m/(m_t r_c)$, $e^{-ikc_s} = e^{-i\pi m/m_t} e^{-2\pi i (m \bmod m_t) s/m_t}$. The sum over cells is then a (real) FFT of length $m_t$ per Legendre degree, and the modes fold onto its frequencies. This is exact, with no window or oversampling as in a NUFFT, and costs O(N log N) instead of O(modes × cells) per axis.
  • In z, there is no Fourier transform and no zero padding. On a level-0 z-cell (side rc) every wide Gaussian is smooth, so its z-convolution is a small exact matrix in the Legendre basis of the cells, computed once by quadrature. For each mode the z-part is $\sum_\ell c_\ell(\dot k) G_\ell$, applied to all modes by one product per Gaussian. This is the idea of the Fourier–Chebyshev solver of the spectral SOG method for quasi-2D systems (Gao, Jiang, Liang, Xu and Zhou), made exact in the cell basis.
  • The mode $\dot k = 0$ takes the kernel of all wide Gaussians, $D(z) = \sum_\ell (\pi w_\ell \delta_\ell/L_1L_2)(e^{-z^2/\delta_\ell} - 1)$, which converges (the tail beyond the u-series is summed in closed form) and tends to the exact $-2\pi|z|/(L_1L_2)$. The constants left out cancel for a neutral density. It plays the role of the separate $k = 0$ term of 2-periodic Ewald summation (Lindbo and Tornberg).

Accuracy and cost

Against a 2-periodic Ewald sum (test/q2d_oracle.jl), the max error is 0.03–0.23 eps relative to $\max|u|$ at eps = 1e-3, 1e-6, 1e-9. The tests cover a square cell, a 2 × 3 cell and a thin 4 × 4 × 0.5 slab (test/test_q2d.jl).

For the 100 × 100 × 1 slab at eps = 1e-6 (rc = 1, 389k modes), the wide part takes 133 ms of 0.84 s at 48 threads. See Performance.
